# 🎼 Cadence Sprint — Adaptive Sight-Reading Trainer for Relentless Musicians

> *"200 wpm or Rachmaninoff."* — the original mantra that started a movement.

Cadence Sprint is a keyboard-first, browser-native practice studio for musicians who want to read music the way speed-readers devour novels: fluidly, unconsciously, and at speed. It is not a game. It is not a toy. It is a metronome for your eyes.

Where traditional sight-reading apps hold your hand and reward you for two correct bars, Cadence Sprint raises the bar until your fingers complain — and then keeps going. It tracks every hesitation, every misread interval, every panicked leap across the staff, and rebuilds tomorrow's practice session around the exact weaknesses you showed it today.

---

## 🚀 What Cadence Sprint Actually Is

Most sight-reading tools behave like polite tutors. Cadence Sprint behaves like a conservatory jury that never sleeps. You open a session, choose a repertoire tier, and the engine begins throwing notation at you in escalating waves. The tempo adapts. The key signatures drift. Accidentals appear like uninvited guests and then leave just as abruptly. Within ten minutes you will have seen more real notation than most casual players see in a week.

The core philosophy is simple: **reading speed is a trained reflex, not a knowledge problem.** You already know what a third looks like. The bottleneck is latency — the gap between recognition and execution. Cadence Sprint exists to crush that gap.

## ✨ Feature Constellation

### 🎯 Adaptive Difficulty Engine
The difficulty engine is not a slider. It is a living system. It watches your note accuracy, your timing variance between beats, and the specific intervals where your eyes stall. Each session's output feeds the next session's input, generating a trajectory curve tuned to your actual ceiling rather than a generic "beginner / intermediate / advanced" dropdown.

### 🌍 Multilingual Notation Vocabulary Support
Staff notation is universal, but the words around it are not. Cadence Sprint ships with interface and terminology localization across a wide range of languages, so a learner in São Paulo, Seoul, or Stockholm can practice using the same musical vocabulary they were taught in. Interval names, dynamic markings, and tempo terms are localized per region rather than dumped through a single translation layer.

### 📱 Responsive Canvas Interface
The rendering surface is built to flex from a phone propped against a music stand to a widescreen desktop rig with a MIDI keyboard attached. Every control, staff line, and tempo dial reflows without breaking layout. If you can see the screen, you can practice on it.

### 🎹 Keyboard and MIDI Input Modes
Play your answers on a physical MIDI controller, a computer keyboard mapped to a virtual piano, or simply tap rhythm responses if you are working on pure notation recognition. The input layer is decoupled from the scoring layer, which means you can drill one skill without the other getting in the way.

### 🕰️ 24/7 Session Availability
Practice at 3 AM before a competition or at noon during a lunch break. The training engine runs entirely client-side, so there is no queue, no server downtime, and no account gate standing between you and the next measure.

### 📊 Longitudinal Progress Ledger
Every session is logged into a local progress ledger that charts reading velocity over weeks and months. You will see the exact moment your plateau broke, and you will see the exact week it came back. That visibility is the entire point.

### 🎵 Repertoire Sourcing Beyond Exercises
Instead of feeding you endless generated scales, Cadence Sprint pulls motif fragments and harmonic patterns inspired by the real literature — from Baroque counterpoint skeletons to late-Romantic chromatic thickets. The exercises feel like music because they are built from the DNA of music.

### 🔁 Miss-and-Replay Loop
When you fumble a passage, the engine quietly marks it and returns to a variation of it later in the session — never the identical bar, always a cousin. This spaced revisitation is how reflexes actually set.

### ♿ Accessible by Default
High-contrast themes, adjustable staff sizes, screen-reader-friendly controls, and reduced-motion modes are baked into the design rather than bolted on.

## 🧠 The Design Metaphor: A Gym for the Eyes

Think of your visual cortex as a muscle group. Most people train it by reading one piece slowly, over and over, until they memorize it — which trains memory, not reading. Cadence Sprint treats reading like interval training: short bursts of unfamiliar notation at slightly too-fast tempos, followed by recovery, followed by another burst. The discomfort is the mechanism. The adaptation is the reward.

## 🎨 Screenshot Walkthrough

A typical Cadence Sprint session unfolds across three panes:

1. **The Staff Pane** — where notation streams across at your current tempo, with a glowing cursor that never waits.
2. **The Telemetry Pane** — where latency spikes, misread intervals, and key-signature disorientation are plotted in real time.
3. **The Debrief Pane** — a post-session summary that reads like a coach's clipboard, not a scoreboard.

Nothing in the interface is decorative. Every pixel either feeds you notation or feeds you feedback.
